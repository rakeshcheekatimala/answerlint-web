# Action policy — v1

Actions exist to improve a high-stakes buyer answer, not a vanity score.

Each action must:

- address an observed accuracy, completeness, differentiation, trust or risk gap;
- link to a supplied owned page;
- name the exact affected prompts and supporting runs;
- include a short checklist that can be reviewed by a human owner;
- define a re-test using the same locked cohort;
- identify the CEO, CFO, product or marketing decision it informs;
- connect the work to demand capture, conversion, retention, trust or risk
  reduction as a testable value hypothesis;
- state the cost of inaction qualitatively unless customer-supplied financial
  data exists; never invent traffic, pipeline, conversion or revenue amounts;
- state an evidence thesis that explains the proposed mechanism as a
  hypothesis, list plausible alternative explanations, and define what result
  would falsify the thesis;
- name tempting but unsupported actions in `do_not_do` so teams do not respond
  with mass content, prompt stuffing, fake authority or an unmeasured redesign;
- distinguish observed customer pain in the supplied answers from inferred
  pain and label the latter as a hypothesis;
- use “associated with” language after a change, never causal language;
- avoid mass content generation, fake reviews, undisclosed outreach, or claims not
  present in the approved brand evidence.
